# What Stripe needs for bank-or-card Pay now with a card fee, and how a bank payment confirms

Research for [#111](https://github.com/bigapejit/expand-handyman-RP/issues/111), part of the Pay now map [#109](https://github.com/bigapejit/expand-handyman-RP/issues/109). Extends [`payment-record-shape.md`](https://github.com/bigapejit/expand-handyman-RP/blob/research/payment-record-shape/docs/research/payment-record-shape.md) (#61): Checkout Session per invoice, `pi_` as the row identity, dedupe on object id, refunds and disputes as events. Checked against Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, Washington and Convex primary sources on 2026-09-23. Every claim links to the page it came from; anything I could not confirm is marked **unverified**.

## Short answer

1. **One session per button.** A Checkout Session's line items are fixed when it is minted, and nothing in hosted Checkout changes the amount by the payment method the customer picks. So "Pay by bank" mints a session with `payment_method_types: ["us_bank_account"]` and "Pay by card" mints one with `["card"]` plus the fee line. Stripe's own surcharge feature exists but is a PaymentIntents preview API and, for automatic card-type detection, a private preview on the Payment Element only; neither works on the hosted page.
2. **The fee is a second `line_items[]` entry with `price_data`** (`currency: "usd"`, `unit_amount`, `product_data.name: "Card fee"`), no Product needed. Checkout shows the name; whether Stripe's standard email receipt itemises it is **unverified** (the docs imply it via `unit_label`; check in a sandbox). Also set `payment_intent_data.description` so the Dashboard and receipt carry "Invoice 1001 plus card fee" whatever the receipt layout does.
3. **Surcharge rules bite in two places.** Visa and Mastercard allow surcharges on **credit** cards only, never debit or prepaid; Visa caps at the lower of 3% and your actual cost; Mastercard caps at cost (4% ceiling); both need 30 days' notice to the acquirer (Stripe), Mastercard also to Mastercard; disclose before the customer chooses and as a separate line on the receipt; return the fee on refund. Washington has no surcharge statute; only the general Consumer Protection Act (RCW 19.86.020) applies. Two problems for the map: (a) hosted Checkout cannot tell debit from credit before the card is entered, so a flat card fee surcharges debit cards, which the network rules forbid; (b) Stripe's card price is 2.9% + 30¢, which is **below 3%** for anything over $300, so a flat 3% exceeds "cost of acceptance" for every invoice the fee applies to. See "What would change the map".
4. **What a bank payer goes through.** Checkout opens Financial Connections: pick the bank (12,000+ supported), consent, log in (OAuth at most banks), pick the account, accept the mandate, Pay. Fallback is manual entry with two micro-deposits (1 to 2 business days, email with a hosted verify link, 10 days and 10 attempts). Events: `checkout.session.completed` with `payment_status: "unpaid"` when the customer authorises, then `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded` (or `async_payment_failed`) when the bank answers. `verification_method: "instant"` removes the micro-deposit path.
5. **Payment on its way** starts on `checkout.session.completed` for a bank session (`payment_status: "unpaid"`) and ends on `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded` (write the payment; its day is the Pacific day of that event) or `checkout.session.async_payment_failed` (a returned debit; clear the state, the invoice reads Unpaid or Overdue again). Standard settlement is T+4 business days, cutoff 21:00 US/Eastern; T+2 is an opt-in. Stripe emails the customer the mandate confirmation and micro-deposit notices; it documents **no** email for a failed one-off debit, so the owner (or the app) tells the customer. A failed debit costs $4; a rare late return after success arrives as a dispute.
6. **"Fully gone" is two events.** Cards and bank alike: `charge.refunded` with `data.object.refunded === true` (a full refund) and `charge.dispute.closed` with `data.object.status === "lost"`. Ignore `charge.refunded` when `refunded` is `false` (partial), ignore `charge.dispute.created` and `funds_withdrawn` (money is on hold, not gone), and ignore `refund.failed` (Stripe says the owner must refund another way). ACH disputes are final, so `charge.dispute.closed` arrives already `lost`.
7. **Convex wiring: plain `stripe` SDK in an `httpAction`, not the component.** `await req.text()` then `stripe.webhooks.constructEventAsync(...)` (Web Crypto; the Convex runtime has it, and the `@convex-dev/stripe` component itself runs exactly this code). The component (v0.1.6) requires a `priceId`, has no handler for `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`, `charge.refunded` or any dispute event, writes its own customer-centric tables and does not dedupe events; it solves subscriptions, not this. Dev testing: register the dev deployment's `https://<name>.convex.site/stripe` URL as a sandbox endpoint, or `stripe listen --forward-to` that URL; set `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` and `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` per deployment with `npx convex env set`.
8. **Test to live.** In a sandbox: card `4242 4242 4242 4242`; bank via the "Test (OAuth)" institution (any login) or manual routing `110000000` with `000123456789` (succeeds), `000222222227` (insufficient funds, fails), `000555555559` (disputed); micro-deposit values `32` and `45` or `SM11AA`; test ACH settles instantly; no customer emails in a sandbox unless the address is `name+test_email@domain`. Going live: live keys, a live webhook endpoint with its own `whsec_`, US bank account switched on in live payment-method settings, "Successful payments" and "Refunds" customer emails on, and the 30-day surcharge notices sent.

## 1. One session per choice

- `payment_method_types` on a Checkout Session is "A list of the types of payment methods (e.g. card) this Checkout Session is allowed to accept" ([Checkout Session object](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/object?query=payment_method_types)). The values are the PaymentMethod `type` enum: `card` ("Card payments are supported through many networks, card brands, and select Link funding sources") and `us_bank_account` ("ACH Direct Debit is used to debit US bank accounts through the Automated Clearing House (ACH) payments system") ([PaymentMethod object](https://docs.stripe.com/api/payment_methods/object)).
- When you omit it, the Dashboard decides: "If you list payment methods manually in an existing integration and want to manage payment methods in your Dashboard, remove `payment_method_types` from your integration code" ([dynamic payment methods](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/payment-methods/dynamic-payment-methods)). The ACH Checkout guide's step 1 is "Enable US bank account in your Dashboard. Stripe automatically displays US bank account to eligible customers using dynamic payment methods. If you currently specify `payment_method_types`, see the migration guide" ([accept an ACH payment, Checkout](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit/accept-a-payment?payment-ui=checkout)). Two filters exist for the Dashboard-managed case: `allowed_payment_method_types` ("acts as a filter on the dynamically computed set of eligible payment methods rather than an explicit static list. Only payment methods that are both dynamically eligible and present in this list will be offered") and `excluded_payment_method_types` ("should only be used when payment methods for this Checkout Session are managed through the Stripe Dashboard") ([create a Checkout Session](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create)).
- **Recommendation:** an explicit `payment_method_types` list per button. If Stripe ever rejects it, `allowed_payment_method_types: ["card"]` / `["us_bank_account"]` with both methods enabled in the Dashboard gives the same result.
- **Can one session carry a fee on cards only? No.** `line_items` are a create-time parameter ("A list of items the customer is purchasing", [create](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create?query=line_items)); `payment_method_options.us_bank_account` and `.card` hold verification, 3DS and instalment settings, no amounts ([create, `payment_method_options`](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create?query=payment_method_options)); Adaptive Pricing is currency localisation ("lets your customers pay in their local currency", [Adaptive Pricing](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/currencies/localize-prices/adaptive-pricing?payment-ui=stripe-hosted)). Stripe's surcharge support is on the PaymentIntents API: "Your integration must be on API version 2026-03-25.preview" and "you need to specify the total amount inclusive of the surcharge in the `amount` field", with the surcharge in `amount_details[surcharge][amount]` ([Collect surcharges](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/cards/surcharge)); `?query=automatic_surcharge` on the Checkout create page returns nothing. Automatic surcharge (card-type detection) "is in private preview", "Only the Payment Element supports automatic surcharge", US only, and needs a third-party app ("Stripe supports Yeeld and InterPayments") ([Automatic surcharge](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/advanced/surcharge)). Hence two buttons, two sessions.
- **ACH in Checkout needs no Stripe Customer.** `customer_creation` defaults to `if_required`: "The Checkout Session will only create a Customer if it is required for Session confirmation. Currently, only `subscription` mode Sessions and `payment` mode Sessions with post-purchase invoices enabled require a Customer"; without one "you can still retrieve email, address, and other customer data entered in Checkout with `customer_details`" ([create](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create)). The ACH guide's Customer step is marked "[Recommended]", not required ([accept an ACH payment, Checkout](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit/accept-a-payment?payment-ui=checkout)). This keeps the map's "no Stripe Customer records".
- Other fields worth setting on both sessions ([create](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create)): `mode: "payment"`; `success_url` with the literal `{CHECKOUT_SESSION_ID}` and `cancel_url` back to the invoice link; `client_reference_id` (max 200 chars) and `metadata.invoiceId`, plus `payment_intent_data.metadata.invoiceId` (refunds and disputes reference the PaymentIntent, not the session; see #61); `expires_at` ("anywhere from 30 minutes to 24 hours", default 24 h); `submit_type: "pay"`. All line items must be `usd` for ACH ("make sure you express Prices for all line items in US dollars", [ACH Checkout](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit/accept-a-payment?payment-ui=checkout)).

## 2. The fee as a line

- `line_items[].price_data` is "Data used to generate a new Price object inline"; `price_data.product_data` is "Data used to generate a new Product object inline"; `product_data.name` is "The product's name, meant to be displayable to the customer"; `unit_amount` is "A non-negative integer in the smallest currency unit representing how much to charge" ([create, `line_items`](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create?query=line_items)). So the card session is two ad hoc lines: `Invoice 1001` at the Amount Due and `Card fee` at the fee, no catalogue objects. Each session creates throwaway Product and Price objects in Stripe; that is normal for `price_data`.
- The hosted page shows them: "Checkout displays the product `name`, `description`, and `images` that you supply" ([accept a payment](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/accept-a-payment?payment-ui=checkout&ui=stripe-hosted)). `product_data.unit_label` "will be included in customers' receipts, invoices, Checkout, and the customer portal" ([create, `line_items`](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create?query=line_items)), which implies receipts list line items, but the [receipts page](https://docs.stripe.com/receipts) does not enumerate what a standard receipt shows: **unverified**, settle it with one sandbox payment and a manual receipt ("If you need to send a receipt for a test payment, send a manual receipt", same page). Belt and braces: `payment_intent_data.description` ("An arbitrary string attached to the object. Often useful for displaying to users", [create, `payment_intent_data`](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create?query=payment_intent_data)) set to something like `Invoice 1001: $1,200.00 plus $36.00 card fee`.
- Receipts are a Dashboard switch: "open Settings > Business > Customer emails. Under Payments, toggle Successful payments or Refunds"; "For payments, receipts are only sent if the payment succeeds"; ACH receipts therefore arrive at confirmation, not at checkout ([receipts](https://docs.stripe.com/receipts)). `payment_intent_data.receipt_email` forces one: "If `receipt_email` is specified for a payment in live mode, a receipt will be sent regardless of your email settings" ([create, `payment_intent_data`](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create?query=payment_intent_data)). Not needed if the switch is on. `invoice_creation` would give an itemised paid invoice but is "priced separately" at 0.4% ([receipts](https://docs.stripe.com/receipts), [pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)); the map rules Stripe Invoicing out and the line item does the job.
- **Nothing built in beats the line item on hosted Checkout.** The native surcharge API (above) is PaymentIntents-only preview. It does say what Stripe expects of any surcharge, which the line item must honour by hand: "Conspicuously disclose surcharge amounts that apply to each transaction and the total costs to the cardholder ahead of purchase, and reflect the surcharge separately on the transaction receipt"; "Provide the ability to cancel the transaction, or choose a different payment method after the surcharge amount is disclosed"; "return the entire surcharge in the event of a full refund" ([Collect surcharges](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/cards/surcharge)). A full refund from the Dashboard refunds the whole charge, fee included, so the last one is automatic. `application_fee_amount` is Connect-only and irrelevant.

## 3. Surcharge rules

**Visa** (U.S. Merchant Surcharge Q&A, version 02152024, [PDF](https://usa.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/global/support-legal/documents/merchant-surcharging-qa-for-web.pdf), linked from [Visa regulations and fees](https://usa.visa.com/support/small-business/regulations-fees.html)):

- "Notify your acquirer at least 30 days prior to commencing surcharging" (the acquirer is Stripe).
- "Limit surcharging to credit cards only (debit cards and prepaid cards cannot be surcharged)"; and on signature debit: "No. The cardholder is still using a Visa debit card."
- "Limit the amount to your merchant discount rate (MDR) for the applicable credit card or 3% whichever is lowest."
- "Disclose the surcharge as a merchant fee and, for both in-store and online transactions, clearly alert consumers to the practice at the point of entry, the point of sale or transaction, and on every receipt"; "must disclose the surcharge as a separate charge on the consumer's transaction receipt".
- Brand level or product level, "but not both"; if you surcharge Visa you "must surcharge Visa on the same terms and conditions as any equal or higher cost competitor".
- "Visa understands that (i) Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts, Oklahoma and Puerto Rico, prohibit surcharging and (ii) Colorado, Minnesota, New Jersey and New York, have requirements for surcharging" (as of 2024-02-15). Washington is not named.
- Enforcement: "the acquirer of any merchant identified as surcharging improperly may be assessed an immediate US $1,000 fine". A cash discount is only legal when the card price is the displayed price: a total "achieved by applying an additional fee for a card payment ... may be treated as, a surcharge".

**Mastercard.** Mastercard's pages block automated fetching (HTTP 403), so these are search-engine extracts of Mastercard's own [merchant surcharge rules](https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/business/support/merchant-surcharge-rules.html) and [FAQ](https://www.mastercard.com/content/dam/mccom/us/business/documents/Merchant-Surcharge-FAQ.pdf); **verify on the page before quoting to a lawyer**: surcharges "are not allowed on Debit Mastercard or Mastercard prepaid cards"; the surcharge "must not be more than the merchant's cost to accept" and "in no event may the merchant surcharge greater than 4%"; "provide advance notice to both Mastercard and the merchant's acquirer ... no less than thirty days before" via the [merchant surcharge form](https://www.mastercard.com/us/en/business/support/merchant-surcharge-rules/form.html); disclose "at the point of interaction, which shall include the amount of the surcharge and the dollar amount of the surcharge on the transaction receipt"; "If a merchant chooses to surcharge credit transactions they cannot assess a convenience fee for Mastercard credit transactions."

**Stripe's own line** ([Collect surcharges](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/cards/surcharge)): availability table "United States | Credit cards only | 3%"; "you must comply with all applicable laws, regulations, and card network rules"; "You're fully responsible for any fines, penalties, or losses arising in connection with your failure to adhere to applicable surcharging requirements." Stripe's US card price is "2.9% + 30¢ per successful transaction for domestic cards" ([pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)).

**Washington.** No statute prohibits or caps surcharges. NCSL's survey lists Washington among states that "permit cash discounts but do not prohibit surcharges on credit/debit card usage" ([NCSL, credit or debit card surcharges statutes](https://www.ncsl.org/financial-services/credit-or-debit-card-surcharges-statutes), last updated 2016-10-13, so treat as a starting point). A 2023 Washington bill, HB 1727, aimed to let *regulated* businesses charge what "unregulated Washington businesses and state agencies currently use", capped at "three percent of the payment amount" with a "no-cost payment option ... disclosed to the debtor at the same time" ([HB 1727 text](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/biennium/2023-24/Htm/Bills/House%20Bills/1727.htm)); it confirms the legislature's own reading that ordinary businesses may surcharge, and whether it passed does not change that. What does apply is the Consumer Protection Act: "Unfair methods of competition and unfair or deceptive acts or practices in the conduct of any trade or commerce are hereby declared unlawful" ([RCW 19.86.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.86.020)), so the fee must be plainly disclosed and not hidden in the price. **Unverified:** no primary Washington source found for a debit-specific state rule; the debit ban is the networks' rule, which is enough.

**What the customer must see before choosing**, combining the above: that a card fee applies, its exact dollar amount and the total, that it is for credit cards, and a free way to pay next to it. Wording that meets that on the link: "Pay by bank, free" and "Pay by card, plus $36.00 card fee (3% credit card surcharge)". Checkout then shows the fee as its own line, and the receipt should show it separately.

**The debit problem.** Stripe only learns the funding type after the card is entered: `payment_method_details.card.funding` is "Card funding type. Can be `credit`, `debit`, `prepaid`, or `unknown`" on the Charge ([Charge object](https://docs.stripe.com/api/charges/object?query=payment_method_details.card.funding)). Hosted Checkout gives no hook to drop a line item once the card is known. The map already says "The 3% fee applies to any card above $500" and rules out telling debit from credit; that is a knowing departure from Visa's and Mastercard's rules, and the Stripe page says the merchant carries the fines. The honest options are in "What would change the map".

**The cost problem.** Visa caps the surcharge at the lower of 3% and your MDR. Stripe's MDR on a $500.01 payment is 2.9% + $0.30 = $14.80 (2.96%); a 3% fee is $15.00. Above $300 a flat 3% is always more than 2.9% + 30¢, so 3% exceeds cost on every invoice the fee applies to. A fee of exactly 2.9% + 30¢, or a flat 2.9%, is within the rule.

## 4. What a bank payer goes through

- On the hosted page the customer picks US bank account and Financial Connections opens: "Select institution: Users select their bank from frequently chosen institutions or by searching more than 12,000 supported banks"; "Provide consent"; "Log in to bank"; "Select accounts"; "Success" ([Financial Connections fundamentals](https://docs.stripe.com/financial-connections/fundamentals)). "Stripe typically defaults the authentication flow to OAuth if it's available at the financial institution", so the customer logs in on the bank's own site or app and "never shares their login credentials with Stripe" (same page). "On mobile, customers are redirected to the bank login. On desktop, bank login appears in a pop-up window" ([ACH Direct Debit](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit)). Instant verification costs "$1.50 per instant bank account validation" on top of the ACH fee ([local payment methods pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing/local-payment-methods)).
- The mandate is Stripe's job: "When you use Checkout or the Payment Element, Stripe displays the required mandate language and records acceptance automatically. No additional integration is required" ([ACH Direct Debit](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit)). Stripe emails it: "if your customer provides a billing email address, Stripe automatically emails your customer the following information: Confirmation of the mandate, per Nacha requirements. Notification if Stripe needs to use microdeposits" (same page).
- **Manual entry fallback.** "By default, collecting bank account payment information uses Financial Connections to instantly verify your customer's account, with a fallback option of manual account number entry and microdeposit verification." "If the customer opts for microdeposit verification instead of Financial Connections, Stripe automatically sends two small deposits to the provided bank account. These deposits can take 1-2 business days to appear on the customer's online bank statement. When the deposits are expected to arrive, the customer receives an email with a link to confirm these amounts and verify the bank account with Stripe. After verification is complete, the payment begins processing." Verification fails when deposits cannot be sent, after "10 failed verification attempts", or if "The customer failed to verify the bank account within 10 days"; then "you can handle the `checkout.session.async_payment_failed` event to contact the customer about placing a new order" ([ACH Checkout](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit/accept-a-payment?payment-ui=checkout)). Because a verification failure surfaces as `async_payment_failed`, the session has already completed by then: `checkout.session.completed` fires at authorisation, before the deposits are verified (inferred from that page; the docs do not spell out the ordering).
- `payment_method_options.us_bank_account.verification_method`: `automatic` "Instant verification with fallback to microdeposits", `instant` "Instant verification", `microdeposits` "Verification using microdeposits" ([create, `payment_method_options`](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create?query=payment_method_options)). "You can optionally require instant bank account verification using the `payment_method_options[us_bank_account][verification_method]` parameter", passing `financial_connections[permissions][0]=payment_method` alongside ([ACH Checkout](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit/accept-a-payment?payment-ui=checkout)). With `instant`, a customer whose bank is not among the 12,000 has no bank option and falls back to card, Zelle or check. **Recommendation:** `instant`. It keeps "Payment on its way" to the T+4 window instead of T+4 plus up to 10 days of unverified limbo, and the app never has to explain micro-deposits.
- **Events, bank session, in order** ([event types](https://docs.stripe.com/api/events/types), [fulfilment](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment?payment-ui=stripe-hosted), [Dashboard payment methods](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/dashboard-payment-methods?payment-ui=stripe-hosted)):

| Moment | Event | On the session |
|---|---|---|
| Customer accepts the mandate and presses Pay | `checkout.session.completed` ("Occurs when a Checkout Session has been successfully completed"); Stripe's ACH table: "The customer has successfully authorized the debit payment ... Wait for the payment to succeed or fail" | `status: "complete"`, `payment_status: "unpaid"` ("The payment funds are not yet available in your account") |
| Same moment | `payment_intent.processing` ("Occurs when a PaymentIntent has started processing") | `payment_intent` set (`pi_`) |
| Bank confirms, T+4 (or T+2) | `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded` ("Occurs when a payment intent using a delayed payment method finally succeeds"), with `payment_intent.succeeded` and `charge.succeeded` | `payment_status: "paid"` ("The payment funds are available in your account") |
| Bank refuses | `checkout.session.async_payment_failed` ("Occurs when a payment intent using a delayed payment method fails"), with `payment_intent.payment_failed` and `charge.failed` | `payment_status` stays `unpaid` |
| Nobody paid within `expires_at` | `checkout.session.expired` | `status: "expired"` |

- A card session skips the middle: `checkout.session.completed` arrives with `payment_status: "paid"`. One handler, one gate: "Check the `payment_status` property to determine if it requires fulfillment" and "Perform fulfillment only once per payment ... your `fulfill_checkout` function might be called multiple times, possibly concurrently" ([fulfilment](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment?payment-ui=stripe-hosted)).
- The customer is redirected to `success_url` right after `completed`, while the bank payment is still processing: "funds won't be immediately available when Checkout completes ... The status of the object is in processing until the payment status either succeeds or fails" (same page). So the page the link shows after a bank checkout is already "Payment on its way".
- A session that expires mid-verification: `expires_at` is 30 minutes to 24 hours, so with `instant` verification it cannot expire mid-payment; with micro-deposits the 10-day verification window outlives the session, one more reason for `instant`. **Unverified:** what Stripe does to a completed session whose micro-deposit verification is still open after 24 hours.

## 5. Payment on its way

- **Start:** `checkout.session.completed` where `data.object.payment_status === "unpaid"` (only a bank session can produce this). Record the `cs_` and `pi_` ids and the invoice in a small pending table, not a payment row (#61, "Money that has not arrived is not a payment").
- **End, paid:** `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`. Write the payment row with `stripePaymentIntentId = data.object.payment_intent`, `source: "stripe"`. The payment's day is the Pacific day of the event's `created` (seconds since epoch) or of `payment_intent.succeeded`; both mark the moment Stripe confirmed. Do not use the session's `created`, which is the day the customer pressed Pay.
- **End, returned:** `checkout.session.async_payment_failed`. Delete the pending record; the invoice reads Unpaid, or Overdue if the due date has passed. The customer cannot retry on that session; pressing Pay again mints a new one. Detail on the failure is on the PaymentIntent (`last_payment_error`) and Charge (`failure_code`, `failure_message`); the charge's `payment_method_details.us_bank_account` carries `bank_name`, `last4`, `expected_debit_date` ("Estimated date to debit the customer's bank account") and, once succeeded, `payment_reference` ("Reference number to locate ACH payments with customer's bank") ([Charge object](https://docs.stripe.com/api/charges/object?query=payment_method_details.card.funding)).
- **Timing.** "Standard settlement (T+4): 4 business days from payment creation, cutoff 21:00 US/Eastern"; "Faster settlement (T+2): 2 business days from payment creation, cutoff 14:00 US/Eastern ... available only to eligible US users. You can check your eligibility and activate this option on the Payment methods settings", at "1.2% per transaction for two-day settlement" against "0.8% ... $5.00 cap" for standard ([ACH Direct Debit](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit), [pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing/local-payment-methods)). The map's "up to 4 business days" matches standard settlement. "ACH Direct Debit transactions can fail any time after the payment is initiated through payment confirmation", for "Insufficient funds", "An invalid account number", "A customer disabling debits from their bank account" ([ACH Direct Debit](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit)). Return reasons are Nacha codes: R01 insufficient funds, R02 account closed, R03 no account, R04 invalid account number, R07 authorization revoked, R10 customer advises not authorized, R29 corporate customer advises not authorized ([Stripe, ACH rejection codes](https://stripe.com/resources/more/the-complete-list-of-ach-rejection-codes-why-they-happen-and-how-to-handle-them)).
- **Late returns after success.** "In rare situations, Stripe might receive an ACH failure from the bank after a PaymentIntent has transitioned to `succeeded`. If this happens, Stripe creates a dispute with a `reason` of: `insufficient_funds`, `incorrect_account_details`, `bank_cannot_process`. Stripe charges a failure fee in this situation." And: "If a payment fails after funds have been made available in your Stripe balance, Stripe immediately removes funds from your Stripe account" ([ACH Direct Debit](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit)). These reach the app as the dispute path in section 6, so a payment already written as Paid can still be removed later.
- **Fees.** "$4.00 for failed payments", "$15.00 for disputed payments" ([pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing/local-payment-methods)). "Debit failure fees aren't charged for retry attempts that we initiate automatically" applies to Billing retries only ([ACH Direct Debit](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit)).
- **Emails.** Documented ACH emails from Stripe to the customer are the mandate confirmation and the micro-deposit notices ([ACH Direct Debit](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit)), the receipt on success ([receipts](https://docs.stripe.com/receipts)) and, for refunds, only when "The original charge was created on a customer in your Stripe account" and "Email customers for refunds" is on ([refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds)); with no Customer object, the refund email will not go out. Stripe's failed-payment emails belong to Billing ("Send emails when card payments fail" under subscription settings, [automatic collection](https://docs.stripe.com/invoicing/automatic-collection)); there is **no documented email for a failed one-off Checkout debit**. The map's open question "whether the customer hears" is therefore the app's or the owner's to answer; Stripe's fulfilment page suggests "you can send an email to your customer when a delayed payment fails" ([fulfilment](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment?payment-ui=stripe-hosted)).

## 6. Refund and lost dispute, full only

- **Full refund, cards and bank alike:** `charge.refunded`, "Occurs whenever a charge is refunded, including partial refunds. Listen to `refund.created` for information about the refund" ([event types](https://docs.stripe.com/api/events/types)). `data.object` is the Charge; `refunded` is `true` only when the whole amount is refunded and `amount_refunded` is the running total (#61, [Charge object](https://docs.stripe.com/api/charges/object)). Rule: on `charge.refunded`, if `data.object.refunded === true`, delete the payment row whose `stripePaymentIntentId` equals `data.object.payment_intent`; otherwise ignore (partial refunds are out of scope). A Dashboard refund defaults to full: "By default, you'll issue a full refund" ([refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds)). Refunds go back "only ... to the original payment method", and "Stripe's processing fees from the original transaction aren't returned" (same page).
- **Bank refunds are slow and can fail.** ACH: "Full refunds" only, "Submission window: 180 days", "Processing time: 3 business days", "can't be canceled", "always processed as a separate credit to the customer's bank account"; "Stripe also notifies you of the final refund status using the `refund.updated` or `refund.failed` webhook event ... If a refund fails, the status transitions to `failed`, Stripe returns the amount to your Stripe balance, and you must arrange an alternative way to provide your customer with a refund" ([ACH Direct Debit](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit)). Since the owner still owes the customer the money, the app should keep the payment removed and not react to `refund.failed`. Cards: a refund shortly after the charge may be a reversal ("the original charge drops off the customer's statement"), which changes nothing for the app ([refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds)).
- **Lost dispute:** `charge.dispute.closed`, "Occurs when a dispute is closed and the dispute status changes to `lost`, `warning_closed`, or `won`" ([event types](https://docs.stripe.com/api/events/types)). Rule: on `charge.dispute.closed`, if `data.object.status === "lost"`, delete the payment row for `data.object.payment_intent`. Cards: `charge.dispute.created` and `charge.dispute.funds_withdrawn` ("Occurs when funds are removed from your account due to a dispute") come first and the money sits withdrawn for the "2-3 months" a card dispute takes (#61); the map chose to act only on the outcome, so those two are ignored. Bank: "Unlike credit card disputes, all ACH Direct Debit disputes are final with no process for appeal" and "When a customer disputes a payment, Stripe sends a `charge.dispute.closed` webhook event, and the PaymentMethod authorization is revoked" ([ACH Direct Debit](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit), [ACH Checkout](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit/accept-a-payment?payment-ui=checkout)); so for a bank payment the same `closed` + `lost` rule fires at once. An ACH proof-of-authorization inquiry arrives as `charge.dispute.created` with `status: "warning_needs_response"` and Stripe answers it itself for hosted mandates ("If you use Stripe-hosted ACH mandates, Stripe automatically responds to the customer's bank with a copy of the debit authorization"); it ends in `closed` as `won` or `lost`, so the rule still holds.
- **Dedupe** on the object: `charge.refunded` can arrive more than once per charge (each further partial refund) and any event can be redelivered; deleting a row that is already gone must be a no-op. Log `evt_` ids too ([webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks)).
- **Idempotency on the Stripe side** is already in #61: `Idempotency-Key` on session creation, keyed on invoice id and amount.

## 7. Convex wiring

**The webhook.** One more route in `convex/http.ts` next to `/thumbtack`:

- "HTTP actions are exposed at `https://<your deployment name>.convex.site`" and "support request and response body types of `.text()`, `.json()`, `.blob()`, and `.arrayBuffer()`" ([Convex HTTP actions](https://docs.convex.dev/functions/http-actions)). Stripe: "Stripe requires the raw body of the request to perform signature verification ... Any manipulation to the raw body of the request causes the verification to fail" ([webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks)). So `const body = await req.text()` and pass that string, untouched.
- "HTTP actions run in the same environment as queries and mutations so also do not have access to Node.js-specific JavaScript APIs" ([Convex HTTP actions](https://docs.convex.dev/functions/http-actions)); the default runtime "supports most npm libraries that work in the browser, Deno, and Cloudflare workers" and has Web Crypto ([Convex runtimes](https://docs.convex.dev/functions/runtimes)). stripe-node's own non-Node example does exactly this: "The .text() method must be used as the verification relies on the raw request body rather than the parsed JSON" then `await stripe.webhooks.constructEventAsync(body, signature, webhookSecret)` ([stripe-node Deno example](https://github.com/stripe/stripe-node/blob/master/examples/webhook-signing/deno/main.ts)). The `@convex-dev/stripe` component runs `new StripeSDK(apiKey)` and `constructEventAsync` inside `httpActionGeneric` with no `"use node"` ([component source, `registerRoutes`](https://github.com/get-convex/stripe/blob/main/src/client/index.ts)), which is the proof it works in the Convex runtime. Use `constructEventAsync`, not `constructEvent`; the sync one needs Node crypto.
- Verification uses the `Stripe-Signature` header; "Our libraries have a default tolerance of 5 minutes between the timestamp and the current time"; "Stripe generates a unique secret key for each endpoint. If you use the same endpoint for both test and live API keys, the secret is different for each one" ([webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks)).
- Return 2xx fast and do the work in one mutation: "Quickly returns a successful status code (2xx) before any complex logic"; Checkout "waits up to 10 seconds for your server to respond to the webhook event delivery before redirecting your customer" ([webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks), [fulfilment](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment?payment-ui=stripe-hosted)). One `ctx.runMutation(internal.payments.applyStripeEvent, { event })` that switches on `event.type`, dedupes by `pi_`/`evt_` and writes, is well inside that. Unlike Thumbtack, a signature failure should answer 400 and an unhandled type 200.
- Subscribe the endpoint to exactly: `checkout.session.completed`, `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`, `checkout.session.async_payment_failed`, `checkout.session.expired`, `charge.refunded`, `charge.dispute.closed`. "Listening for extra events (or all events) puts undue strain on your server and we don't recommend it" ([webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks)). If the books effort later wants fees, add `charge.updated` (#61).
- Delivery: at-least-once, unordered, retried "for up to three days with an exponential back off in live mode" and "three times over the course of a few hours" in a sandbox ([webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks)). `async_payment_succeeded` can arrive before `completed` has been processed if the first delivery failed; the mutation must handle "paid" for a session it has not seen (write the payment straight away) and "unpaid completed" for a session already paid (no-op).

**Minting the session.** A Convex action calls `stripe.checkout.sessions.create(...)` with the app's `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` and returns `session.url`; the component's `createCheckoutSession` does this from an `ActionCtx` without `"use node"` ([component source](https://github.com/get-convex/stripe/blob/main/src/client/index.ts)). If bundling ever objects, the action alone can move to a `"use node"` file; the webhook cannot ("files with the 'use node' directive should not contain any Convex queries or mutations", [Convex runtimes](https://docs.convex.dev/functions/runtimes)), and does not need to.

**Component or SDK.** `@convex-dev/stripe` v0.1.6 ([README](https://github.com/get-convex/stripe/blob/main/README.md), [source](https://github.com/get-convex/stripe/blob/main/src/client/index.ts), [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/get-convex/stripe/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)):

| | Component | Plain `stripe` SDK |
|---|---|---|
| Checkout creation | `createCheckoutSession(ctx, { priceId: string, mode, successUrl, cancelUrl, ... params })`: `priceId` is required and becomes `line_items: [{ price }]`; ad hoc `price_data` only by overriding `line_items` through `params`, with a dummy `priceId` | `stripe.checkout.sessions.create({ line_items: [{ price_data }, ...] })` directly |
| Webhook | `registerRoutes(http, components.stripe, { events, onEvent })`, default path `/stripe/webhook`, verifies with `constructEventAsync` | ~40 lines in `convex/http.ts`, same verification |
| Events it handles | `customer.*`, `customer.subscription.*`, `checkout.session.completed`, `invoice.*`, `payment_intent.succeeded`; `default: console.log("Unhandled event type")`. No `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`, no `charge.refunded`, no `charge.dispute.*` (custom `events` handlers may be attached for any `Stripe.Event.Type`, but the component does nothing for them) | whatever the switch says |
| Tables it writes | its own `customers`, `subscriptions`, `checkout_sessions`, `payments` (`stripePaymentIntentId, amount, currency, status, ...`), `invoices`, keyed on Stripe Customers | the app's `payments` table, keyed on invoice |
| Event dedupe | none in source | the app's index lookup in one mutation (OCC, #61) |
| Stripe API version | pins `2026-04-22.dahlia`; peer `stripe ^22.2.1` | the app picks |

Verdict: **plain SDK**. The component is built around Customers and subscriptions, would write a parallel `payments` table the app does not read, and still needs custom handlers for four of the six events. Its value here is as a worked example of the SDK running in the Convex runtime.

**Local testing.** Convex dev deployments are cloud deployments with a public `.convex.site` URL; a local backend "has no public URL" and cannot receive webhooks ([Convex local deployments](https://docs.convex.dev/cli/local-deployments)). Two ways, both fine:

1. Register `https://<dev-deployment>.convex.site/stripe` as a sandbox webhook endpoint in Workbench ("Registered webhook endpoints must be publicly accessible HTTPS URLs", [webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks)); copy its `whsec_` into the dev deployment. Same shape as production; nothing to keep running.
2. `stripe listen --forward-to https://<dev-deployment>.convex.site/stripe`: `--forward-to <url>` is "The URL that snapshot webhook events will be forwarded to", any URL, with `--skip-verify` for self-signed HTTPS; "The webhook signing secret provided will not change between restarts to the `listen` command"; "You don't need to configure any webhook endpoints in your Dashboard to receive webhooks with the CLI" ([Stripe CLI listen](https://docs.stripe.com/cli/listen)). Add `--events checkout.session.completed,...` to mirror production. `stripe trigger checkout.session.completed` fires fixtures for a smoke test ([webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks)). Note "If you're using the Stripe CLI for local testing, Checkout redirects to the `success_url` immediately" instead of waiting for the handler ([fulfilment](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment?payment-ui=stripe-hosted)).

Secrets: "environment variables are set per-deployment, you can use different values for the same key in dev and prod deployments"; `npx convex env set STRIPE_SECRET_KEY 'sk_test_...'` and `npx convex env set STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET 'whsec_...'`; read with `process.env` in the action and the HTTP action; `CONVEX_SITE_URL` is provided for building the webhook URL ([Convex environment variables](https://docs.convex.dev/production/environment-variables)). The Vercel build already deploys Convex alongside (commit `ba8f7d8`), so production only needs the live values set once on the prod deployment.

## 8. Test mode to live

**A test payment, end to end** ([testing](https://docs.stripe.com/testing), [ACH Checkout](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit/accept-a-payment?payment-ui=checkout), [Financial Connections testing](https://docs.stripe.com/financial-connections/testing), [testing use cases](https://docs.stripe.com/testing-use-cases)):

1. Sandbox keys on the dev deployment (`sk_test_`, `whsec_` from step 1 or 2 above). Stripe: "For new integrations, use general sandboxes instead of your test mode sandbox" ([sandboxes](https://docs.stripe.com/sandboxes)); a general sandbox isolates Dashboard settings, the legacy test mode shares them with live ("If you change settings in the Dashboard while in the test mode sandboxes, you might also change them in live mode").
2. A throwaway invoice on dev, sent to `owner+test_email@domain` so Stripe's ACH emails actually go out: "use the following email format ... `{username}+test_email@{domain}` ... If you don't include the `+test_email` suffix, Stripe won't send the email". Otherwise "By default, Stripe doesn't email customers in sandboxes"; receipts are never sent for test payments ("send a manual receipt", [receipts](https://docs.stripe.com/receipts)).
3. **Card:** `4242 4242 4242 4242`, any future expiry, any CVC. Decline: `4000 0000 0000 0002`; insufficient funds: `4000 0000 0000 9995`. Expect `checkout.session.completed` with `payment_status: "paid"` and a payment row.
4. **Bank, instant:** choose an institution named "Test (OAuth)" or "Test (Non-OAuth)"; "Entering any input value simulates a successful login". "Invalid Payment Accounts" holds accounts "unusable for ACH payments"; "Down (Unscheduled)" simulates an outage.
5. **Bank, manual (if `automatic` is kept):** routing `110000000`; `000123456789` "The payment succeeds"; `000222222227` "The payment fails due to insufficient funds"; `000111111113` "account is closed"; `000555555559` "The payment triggers a dispute"; `000000000009` "stays in processing indefinitely". "Before test transactions can complete, you need to verify all test accounts": micro-deposit amounts `32` and `45`, or descriptor code `SM11AA`; `10`/`11` (`SM33CC`) simulates too many attempts; `40`/`41` (`SM44DD`) a timeout.
6. Expect `checkout.session.completed` with `payment_status: "unpaid"` ("Payment on its way"), then `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded` or `async_payment_failed`. "Test transactions settle instantly and are added to your available test balance. This behavior differs from live mode, where transactions can take multiple days to settle." **Unverified:** the exact delay before the async event in a sandbox; expect seconds to minutes, not days.
7. Refund and dispute from the sandbox Dashboard; expect `charge.refunded` (`refunded: true`) and, with `000555555559`, `charge.dispute.closed` (`lost`).

**What flips when going live** ([API keys](https://docs.stripe.com/keys), [go-live checklist](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/checklist/go-live), [webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks), [testing use cases](https://docs.stripe.com/testing-use-cases)):

- Keys: "Each mode has its own set of API keys, and objects in one mode aren't accessible to the other"; live keys start `sk_live_`/`pk_live_`. Set them on the prod deployment only.
- Webhook: a second endpoint at `https://<prod-deployment>.convex.site/stripe` created in live mode, with its own `whsec_` ("If you use the same endpoint for both test and live API keys, the secret is different for each one"). Retries become three days.
- Account: "To start accepting real payments, you need to set up your Stripe account, exit your sandbox with the account picker, and use the live API keys". ACH: "you can enable ACH Direct Debit directly from your payment method settings" and "Your account might be reviewed after activation to confirm eligibility" ([ACH Direct Debit](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/ach-direct-debit)); check US bank account is on in live settings, and whether T+2 is wanted.
- Emails: Customer emails "Successful payments" and "Refunds" on in live ([receipts](https://docs.stripe.com/receipts)); the owner's own payment notifications under the Dashboard's email settings.
- Settlement: live ACH takes T+4, so the first live bank payment is the first real "Payment on its way".
- Surcharge notices: Visa's 30-day notice to the acquirer and Mastercard's to Mastercard and the acquirer (section 3). How to notify Stripe as acquirer is **unverified** (no support article found; ask Stripe support), and "Notify your acquirer or card network of your intent to apply a surcharge, if required by your jurisdiction or the card network" is on Stripe's compliance list ([Collect surcharges](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/cards/surcharge)).
- Nothing in the app depends on Stripe objects created in test: `price_data` sessions create no reusable catalogue ("Stripe objects created in a sandbox environment ... aren't usable in live mode").

## What would change the map

1. **A flat 3% card fee breaks two network rules the map cannot route around on hosted Checkout.** (a) Debit and prepaid cards may not be surcharged (Visa Q&A, Mastercard, Stripe's own table "Credit cards only"), and the hosted page cannot tell funding type before the card is entered; the map's "The 3% fee applies to any card above $500" is a decision to surcharge debit cards. (b) Visa caps at the lower of 3% and cost; Stripe's cost is 2.9% + 30¢, which is under 3% for every amount above $300, so 3% is over the cap on every invoice the fee touches. Options, cheapest first: set the fee to **2.9% + 30¢** (or 2.9%) and word it as a credit-card surcharge while accepting the debit exposure; or drop the fee and raise prices (a cash discount is only legal when the card price is the listed price); or move card payment to Stripe's Payment Element with the surcharge preview, which detects funding type but is preview software and a third-party app, against the map's "keep it simple". The owner should hear the $1,000-per-incident fine figure from Visa's Q&A before choosing.
2. **Bank verification: choose `instant`.** The map's "up to 4 business days" holds only if micro-deposits are off. With `automatic`, an unverified manual entry can sit up to 10 days before `async_payment_failed`, and the customer gets Stripe emails about deposits the app never mentions. Cost: a customer whose bank is not among the 12,000 has no bank button.
3. **"Payment on its way" needs a place to live that is not a payment row**, as #61 said: a small pending-session record keyed on `cs_`/`pi_` with the invoice, created on `completed` (`unpaid`) and deleted on `async_payment_succeeded` (after the payment row is written) or `async_payment_failed`. The link and panel read it; Overdue is suppressed while it exists.
4. **A returned debit has no Stripe email.** The "Not yet specified" item "whether the customer hears" cannot be delegated to Stripe: nothing is documented for a failed one-off Checkout debit. Either the map accepts "the owner tells them" or the app sends one email, which #66 ruled out. Also decide the day Overdue counts from; Stripe offers nothing here.
5. **A payment can be removed weeks after it was written.** A rare late ACH return arrives as a dispute after `succeeded`, and card disputes close months later; both come through `charge.dispute.closed` as `lost`. The map's "removes the payment, so the invoice reads Unpaid again" already covers it; the spec should say the Paid stamp can come off an invoice the owner thought closed, and that a voided invoice with a later `lost` dispute is a state to define.
6. **No refund email without a Stripe Customer.** Stripe's refund email requires the charge to be on a Customer; the map's "no Stripe Customer records" means the receipt is the only Stripe email the customer gets. Fine, but say so.
7. **Which events, exactly, so the spec is unambiguous:** `checkout.session.completed`, `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`, `checkout.session.async_payment_failed`, `checkout.session.expired`, `charge.refunded` (act only when `refunded === true`), `charge.dispute.closed` (act only when `status === "lost"`). Plain `stripe` SDK in `convex/http.ts`, `constructEventAsync`, one mutation per event, dedupe by `pi_` and `evt_`.
